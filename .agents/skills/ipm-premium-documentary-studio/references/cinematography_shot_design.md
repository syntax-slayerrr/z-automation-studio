## 1. Directorial principle
Every shot answers: *what should the viewer notice, understand, or feel here, and why is this the best visual form?* A shot without a story function is cut, however beautiful.

## 2. Shot fields
Shot ID and class (hero / standard / utility); duration; narrative beat and evidence IDs; visual truth class; subject and action; location, period, weather, time of day; shot size and angle; lens character and depth of field; camera movement with start and end positions; composition and screen direction; blocking and action beats; lighting motivation and palette; environment interaction; transition and edit point; continuity dependencies; generation prompt and negative constraints; review criteria. Use `templates/shot_card.md` for hero shots and the shot table for others.

## 3. Shot grammar
- Extreme wide / wide: geography, scale, social context.
- Medium: human activity and relationships.
- Close-up: decision-relevant detail, emotion, evidence.
- Insert / macro: documents, hands, objects (only if historically supported).
- Over-the-shoulder: perspective and attention, without inventing a real meeting.
- Tracking / lateral move: movement through a place or process.
- Dolly-in: increasing attention or significance.
- Pull-back: reveal of context or consequence.
- Locked-off: lets evidence or a moment register.
- Elevated / crane move: geography or transition, used sparingly.

## 4. Camera signatures and opening visual modes
**Series lens language is constant; each episode gets one distinguishing camera idea** (its `camera_signature` in the matrix), used for the hero shots and echoed lightly.

`camera_signature` vocabulary: `locked_observational` · `slow_lateral_track` · `reveal_pullback` · `dolly_toward_detail` · `match_cut_driven` · `elevated_glide` · `subtle_handheld` (only when the situation motivates it).

`opening_visual_mode` vocabulary: `place_wide` · `human_action` · `object_insert` · `map_graphic` · `abstract_symbolic` · `text_document`.

Neither repeats in consecutive episodes. Pick the signature that serves the structure: `reverse_reveal` → `reveal_pullback`; `object_doorway` → `dolly_toward_detail`; `quiet_chronicle` → `locked_observational`; `map_movement` → `slow_lateral_track` or `elevated_glide`.

## 5. Camera discipline
- Movement is motivated and physically plausible; avoid perpetual push-ins, orbiting, unmotivated handheld, and impossible indoor drone moves.
- Keep screen direction consistent across cuts unless a transition deliberately resets geography.
- Maintain readable silhouettes and subject separation on small screens.
- Avoid shallow depth of field that hides period detail; use consistent lens language within a sequence.
- A camera move must start and end in a composed frame, not just sweep.

## 6. Vertical 9:16
- Compose for a phone, not a cropped landscape master.
- Keep key action inside the central safe area; leave upper and lower zones for captions and platform UI.
- Use layered depth without obscuring the subject.
- Avoid tiny crowds in wide shots; use layered movement or several connected views.
- Prefer vertical-native compositions: tall architecture, figure-and-doorway, stairs, hanging objects, reflections.
- Test at actual phone size, not just on a monitor.

## 7. Narration sync
- The image arrives **just before** the word that explains it (roughly a quarter to half second).
- The turn line gets a shot of its own: a hold or a slow reveal with space for the pause.
- Never let a shot's most important action happen under a caption or during a loud sound cue.
- Match cut points to narration breaths and to motion peaks.

## 8. Shot count, length, and pacing
Do not target a fixed number of shots. Use the minimum number that gives visual progression. Short inserts and longer observational shots both belong; duration follows story and the rhythm curve. Prefer a few excellent, readable shots over many weak ones. Each cut must add information, perspective, rhythm, or emphasis. The hero shots (3–5) are designed first; the rest are built around them.

## 9. Transitions
Transitions express a relationship: match cut (shape, gesture, or object links time or ideas); sound bridge; graphic or map transition; temporal dissolve; hard cut for contrast. Avoid transition packs and decorative effects.
