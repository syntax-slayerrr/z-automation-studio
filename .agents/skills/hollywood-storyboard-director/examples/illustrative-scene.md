# Illustrative Example — Turning an Abstract Beat into a Board

> This is a fictional teaching example. It is not a claim about a real IPM event or historical scene.

## Input beat

“An organization is preparing to make an important decision. The room is tense; the choice will affect the direction of the group.”

## Weak, generic approach

1. Wide shot of a meeting room.
2. Medium shot of a person speaking.
3. Close-up of a serious face.
4. Slow zoom into a document.
5. Dramatic music and fade out.

Why it fails: it has no specific point of view, no meaningful action, no visual progression, and no clear reason for the camera choices.

## Directed interpretation

**Dramatic idea:** the decision is not abstract; the weight of it is shared by the people in the room.  
**Audience effect:** move from observing a meeting to feeling the responsibility behind the decision.  
**Visual strategy:** show collective attention, then isolate the physical moment when the decision becomes real.  
**Historical status:** all room details and actions in this example are fictional staging.

## Example shot design

### DEMO-SQ01-SH001 — The room holds its breath

- **Purpose:** establish that the decision belongs to a group, not just one speaker.
- **Audience focus:** a table of people whose attention has converged on one unseen point.
- **Shot scale / angle:** wide, slightly elevated but not overhead.
- **Composition:** table creates a strong directional line toward the empty space at its center; foreground shoulders frame the group.
- **Blocking:** no one moves at first; one person shifts a hand toward a paper, then stops.
- **Camera:** locked-off. The stillness is the point.
- **Lighting:** controlled, soft side light; restrained contrast.
- **Sound:** room tone, faint chair movement; music withheld.
- **Edit:** hold long enough for the viewer to register the collective attention.
- **Complexity:** Tier A / B depending on character count.

### DEMO-SQ01-SH002 — The choice becomes tangible

- **Purpose:** turn the abstract decision into a readable action.
- **Audience focus:** the hand placing a marked paper at the center of the table.
- **Shot scale / angle:** close insert, eye-line-neutral tabletop angle.
- **Composition:** paper is the only sharp focal point; hands enter from frame edge without obscuring the mark.
- **Action:** hand approaches, hesitates for a beat, then places the paper flat. Fingers release and remain still.
- **Camera:** static; no push-in needed.
- **Sound:** paper contact is clear; room tone briefly falls away.
- **Edit:** cut on the release, then allow the reaction to carry the next beat.
- **Complexity:** Tier A.

### DEMO-SQ01-SH003 — The group absorbs the result

- **Purpose:** show consequence through reaction rather than explanatory narration.
- **Audience focus:** a few distinct reactions, not a wall of identical serious faces.
- **Shot scale / angle:** medium group composition with layered depth.
- **Blocking:** one person exhales; another looks down; a third meets the speaker's eyes. Reactions are offset, not synchronized.
- **Camera:** very subtle lateral drift only if the production pipeline supports it; otherwise static.
- **Sound:** room tone returns; music may enter after the first reaction.
- **Edit:** use the strongest reaction to lead into the next sequence.
- **Complexity:** Tier B.

## What makes this more directed

- Each shot has a distinct job.
- The camera is not moving by default.
- The central action is staged and readable.
- The sequence progresses from collective context to tangible choice to human consequence.
- The board can be produced with a modest number of assets.
