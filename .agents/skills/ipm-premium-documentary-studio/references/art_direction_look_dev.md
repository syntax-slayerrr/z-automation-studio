## 1. Identity
**Format:** serialized animated historical documentary.
**Visual signature:** premium realistic-stylized 3D, tactile period environments, human-scale storytelling, precise cinematography, restrained color design.
**Audience experience:** immersive, clear, curious, emotionally engaged, intellectually honest.

## 2. What the series is / is not
It IS: a documentary whose historical world is reconstructed in 3D; cinematically directed, not merely illustrated; visually rich but editorially restrained; human, tactile, atmospheric, and specific to time and place.

It is NOT: a children's cartoon, a game cutscene, a generic AI montage, a corporate profile, propaganda, a slideshow, hyperrealistic fake archive, a string of random "epic" shots, or one grade (sepia, orange-teal, fog) applied to everything.

## 3. Realistic-stylized balance
- Credible anatomy and proportions; subtle design simplification is acceptable.
- Natural skin response and varied facial features, no plastic or waxy finish.
- Cloth with believable weave, folds, weight, and period construction.
- Wood, paper, metal, glass, concrete, soil, and vegetation with differentiated material response.
- Avoid over-sharpening, exaggerated pores, uncanny photorealism, glossy toy surfaces, and exaggerated animation.

## 4. Look lock (series-level) and era palettes
Decide at gate S2 and lock after the pilot. The look lock has **constants** and **variables**.

**Constants (never change between episodes)**
- Rendering style and material response; skin and eye treatment.
- Lens family (e.g., a small set of focal-length characters used consistently) and depth-of-field philosophy.
- Frame rate, motion-blur feel, grain level, sharpening, halation: the **glue pass**.
- Caption typography, title-card and end-card format, map and timeline style.
- Safe areas for 9:16.

**Variables (per era or per episode)**
- **Era palette:** a base palette per historical period, derived from evidence of materials, paints, textiles, and lighting conditions, not from a fashionable "vintage" filter. Record the palette as a swatch set with names and usage rules.
- Time-of-day and weather character.
- Dominant materials and set dressing for the place.
- The episode's signature image (see `animation_direction.md`).

Fill the era palette table in the Series Bible after research: era, location types, dominant materials, palette swatches, lighting character, wardrobe notes, things to avoid. Do **not** pre-decide palettes before researching the period.

## 5. Color and light
- Daylight: physically motivated, readable, natural contrast.
- Interiors: practical sources and window direction must make sense.
- Night: preserve detail; never crush blacks for fake drama.
- Historical warmth comes from real materials and light, not an indiscriminate sepia overlay.
- Maintain skin-tone integrity and caption contrast in every grade.
- Avoid constant volumetric fog, teal-orange, and uniformly dark treatment.

## 6. Composition and visual rhythm
- Use foreground, midground, background depth; keep the subject silhouette readable on a phone.
- Preserve caption-safe zones; keep faces, documents, and key action clear of platform UI.
- Use deliberate negative space where typography is needed.
- Alternate visual modes when motivated: place; human action; object/document; map/timeline; archival material; graphic explanation. Do not repeat shot size and camera move for long; variation must clarify, not distract.
- **First frame is a poster.** The opening image should work as a thumbnail and stop a scroll.

## 7. On-screen graphics, typography, maps
- Minimal, editorial, legible; typography is information design, not decoration.
- Dates, names, places, and source labels are verified and added in post.
- No fake newspaper headlines, fake letterheads, gibberish writing, fabricated seals, or pseudo-archival graphics.
- Maps: simple, accurate, labelled as schematic where boundaries or routes are approximate; animate to show movement or reach, not for decoration.
- Reconstruction labels: a small, consistent, unobtrusive label wherever a shot is a contextual or evidence-based reconstruction, per `historical_evidence_protocol.md`.

## 8. Reference board
Per episode, cover: period architecture and streetscape; wardrobe and textile; furniture, transport, tools, paper, signage; lighting and atmosphere; character design and animation language; cinematography and framing. Record source, date, provenance, rights, and whether each item is *reference-only* or *approved visual evidence*.
