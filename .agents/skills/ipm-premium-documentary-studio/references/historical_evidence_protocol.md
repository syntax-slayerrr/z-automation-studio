# Historical Evidence Protocol

## Principle
Historical truth is a production constraint, not a decorative layer. A scene can be visually beautiful and still fail if it implies an unsupported fact.

## Source hierarchy
Prioritize:
1. Contemporary primary documents, official resolutions, minutes, letters, period publications, photographs, and archival records.
2. Official institutional histories and publications with traceable references.
3. Peer-reviewed academic work, scholarly books, and reputable historians.
4. Credible journalism and secondary reference works.
5. Unverified social posts, reposted graphics, and unattributed claims are leads only, never sufficient evidence for key claims.

Official institutional sources are important but still require source criticism. Record authorship, publication date, purpose, and possible institutional perspective.

## Evidence ledger fields
For every claim:
- Claim ID.
- Exact claim in plain language.
- Claim type: date / person / place / decision / cause / quote / statistic / visual detail.
- Source title, author/institution, publication date.
- URL or archive identifier.
- Exact supporting passage/page.
- Source class: primary / institutional secondary / academic / journalism / lead.
- Corroborating sources.
- Contradictions or uncertainty.
- Confidence: verified / probable / disputed / unknown.
- Allowed narration wording.
- Allowed visual implication.
- Rights / credit notes.

## Confidence rules
- **Verified:** directly supported by a strong primary source or multiple independent high-quality sources.
- **Probable:** credible support exists, but direct evidence or corroboration is incomplete.
- **Disputed:** credible sources materially disagree.
- **Unknown:** evidence is insufficient.
Do not convert probable, disputed, or unknown claims into definitive narration.

## Visual truth classes
Label each planned visual:
- ARCHIVAL: genuine historical material with provenance and rights status.
- EVIDENCE-BASED RECONSTRUCTION: visual details supported by evidence.
- CONTEXTUAL RECONSTRUCTION: period-appropriate general context, not a claim that a specific documented event happened exactly this way.
- SYMBOLIC: visual metaphor, clearly not literal historical depiction.
- UNKNOWN / DO NOT DEPICT: unsupported specifics.

## Prohibited fabrications
Never invent:
- A quote, dialogue, inner thought, motive, or private conversation.
- A named attendee, exact room, meeting sequence, banner, logo placement, document, signature, or ceremonial action.
- A historical person's face or likeness without reliable reference and rights/ethical clearance.
- A specific action as fact merely because it makes a better scene.
- A precise date, place, number, or causal claim without evidence.

## Honest gaps are story assets
Unknowns and contested points are not embarrassments to hide; they are the strongest *truthful* source of curiosity. When a gap is real, record it in the ledger as `unknown` or `disputed`, write the allowed narration wording (e.g., "Sumber-sumber berbeda dalam hal ini"), and tell the script stage so it can be used as a hook or a turn (`script_and_curiosity_engine.md` §1). Never fill a gap with invention to make a better scene.

## Where to look (leads, not guaranteed holdings)
Official organizational archives, congress/conference resolutions and proceedings, period publications and periodicals (for example *Suara Muhammadiyah*), national archives and libraries (ANRI, Perpustakaan Nasional), university repositories and peer-reviewed journals, scholarly books on Muhammadiyah and Indonesian student/youth movements, and oral histories. Treat oral history and memoir as valuable but retrospective: corroborate dates and causal claims. Record a dated **search log** (query, source, result, usefulness) so research can be audited and resumed by another researcher or AI.

## Batch research for a 15-episode series
At gate S1, build a **dossier-level** chronology and source map for the whole arc *before* deep-diving any one episode. This reveals contradictions between episodes, shared sources, strong candidates for hooks and turns, and where evidence is thin (amber/red readiness). Deep research then proceeds per episode, in waves, using the same ledger so IDs remain unique across the series (e.g., `E03-C012`).

## Handling gaps
When evidence is missing:
1. Search for corroboration.
2. Narrow the claim.
3. Use a contextual or symbolic visual.
4. Use a narrator's uncertainty where relevant.
5. Omit the detail if it remains misleading.

## Historical sequence to investigate, not assume
Potential milestones commonly associated with IPM history include early student initiatives in Garut (1958), the Muhammadiyah Youth Congress in Yogyakarta (1960), the establishment milestone conventionally associated with Surakarta (1961), the 1992 change to IRM, and restoration of the IPM name in 2008. Treat all exact dates, forum names, resolutions, and causal wording as research questions until supported by authoritative evidence.

## Final audit
For every factual sentence and historically specific visual, ask:
- What exactly supports this?
- Is the source contemporary or retrospective?
- Is the interpretation stronger than the evidence?
- Could a viewer mistake reconstruction for archive?
- Does the shot imply a person, action, or object not established by evidence?
