# Animation Direction

## 1. Design performance, not just movement

A character's action should reveal intention. For each performance beat define:
- **thought / intention:** what the character wants or understands;
- **preparation:** the anticipation before action, when appropriate;
- **primary action:** the main readable movement;
- **reaction:** the response from the character or environment;
- **settle:** how the action resolves;
- **secondary motion:** overlap and follow-through that supports the primary action.

Not every action needs all stages. A quick glance may be enough. Choose timing based on meaning.

## 2. Timing and spacing

Timing determines duration; spacing determines how movement progresses through that duration. Describe:
- approximate duration in seconds or frames when the pipeline requires it;
- acceleration / deceleration or held moments;
- the point of strongest emphasis;
- pauses and reaction windows;
- whether movement is smooth, abrupt, restrained, heavy, light, hesitant, or decisive.

Avoid vague direction such as “slowly moves.” Say what begins slowly, where it accelerates, when it stops, and what the audience should notice.

## 3. Staging for readability

- Keep the primary action unobstructed.
- Use clear silhouettes and distinct poses.
- Separate overlapping characters where possible.
- Avoid simultaneous gestures that compete for attention.
- Direct the character's eyeline before or during the action so the audience can follow intent.
- Use holds at key poses when the viewer needs time to understand an image.

## 4. Principles to apply selectively

- **Anticipation:** prepare the audience for an action.
- **Staging:** present the idea clearly.
- **Arcs:** use believable or stylistically consistent paths.
- **Follow-through / overlap:** secondary parts continue or settle after the main body.
- **Slow in / slow out:** vary spacing for weight and intention.
- **Timing:** control pace, weight, and emotional emphasis.
- **Exaggeration:** clarify performance without breaking the chosen realism level.
- **Secondary action:** support the primary action without competing with it.
- **Appeal:** create distinctive, readable design and performance—not generic prettiness.

For realistic-stylized historical animation, keep gestures grounded and readable. Reserve overt exaggeration for moments where the visual style permits it.

## 5. Environment and atmospheric animation

Environmental motion should have a physical source and a hierarchy:
- primary movement: an object, person, vehicle, paper, curtain, or other story-relevant element;
- secondary movement: wind in fabric, foliage, dust, smoke, water, light flicker, or crowd rhythm;
- atmospheric movement: subtle particles, depth haze, or light variation.

Do not animate everything at once. Let the environment support the subject.

## 6. Hybrid and 2.5D production

When a shot uses generated stills or layered illustrations:
- define foreground, subject, midground, and background layers;
- state which layers move and which remain locked;
- specify parallax direction and approximate intensity;
- prevent the camera move from revealing missing areas or broken perspective;
- avoid warping faces, hands, typography, logos, and historical artifacts;
- keep a stable horizon and consistent light direction.

A useful 2.5D move often needs only a subtle foreground drift and a slower background drift. More movement is not necessarily better.

## 7. Crowd direction

Crowds need an observable pattern:
- number / density as an approximate production requirement;
- dominant movement direction;
- foreground hero extras versus background loops;
- focal subject and readable negative space;
- repeated cycles and variation strategy;
- whether individual faces need detail.

Do not ask for a huge crowd if a small number of well-staged figures communicates the event more clearly.

## 8. Animation-ready action line

Use this concise construction:

> **Start pose → anticipation → primary action → reaction / follow-through → settle.** Duration: __. Main subject: __. Secondary motion: __. Camera: __. Readability priority: __.

## 9. Avoid fake motion

Do not prescribe continuous zoom, floating particles, random camera shake, constant character bobbing, or endless cloth movement merely to avoid a static frame. Stillness is a valid directorial decision.
