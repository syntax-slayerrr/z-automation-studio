# Continuity and Editing

## 1. Continuity is a system

Track continuity across shots and sequences:
- character identity, costume, hair, age / appearance, and accessories;
- prop ownership, placement, condition, and orientation;
- location geography and entrances / exits;
- screen direction and axis;
- eyelines and subject relationships;
- time of day, key-light direction, shadow direction, and weather;
- color palette and atmosphere;
- movement state at the cut;
- typography, logos, maps, documents, and graphic design.

When a sequence spans time, define which changes are intentional and which details remain stable.

## 2. Screen direction and action continuity

For a movement that crosses a frame, make sure its direction remains coherent across cuts unless the sequence intentionally resets geography. Preserve the relationship between a subject's action and the next shot's response.

Use match-on-action when it improves continuity. Ensure the action begins and ends at compatible positions and timing.

## 3. Shot-to-shot transitions

A cut should do at least one of these:
- advance action;
- shift point of view;
- reveal new information;
- create a contrast;
- emphasize a reaction;
- alter time or place;
- create an intentional pause or thematic connection.

Use transitions such as hard cut, match cut, dissolve, graphic match, sound bridge, or motivated camera movement only when their function is clear.

## 4. Pacing

Pacing is not just short versus long shots. It is the rate at which the audience receives information and emotional change.

Vary:
- information density;
- shot duration;
- movement versus stillness;
- wide context versus intimate detail;
- sound density versus silence;
- action versus reaction.

A shot can be short but confusing, or long but gripping. Do not shorten every shot to chase social-media speed. Use duration to preserve comprehension and emotional impact.

## 5. Timecode discipline

Give each shot an estimated duration and time range. The total should add up to the target runtime, including title cards, transitions, pauses, and end card when applicable. If narration timing is not supplied, mark timing as an estimate and do not claim frame-accurate synchronization.

Use timecode format `00:00–00:04` for seconds-based boards. For frame-accurate work, ask for frame rate or state the assumed rate.

## 6. Sound bridges and silence

Sound can connect images across cuts. Consider:
- room tone / environmental bed;
- specific action sounds;
- restrained musical entry or exit;
- a sound bridge across time or location;
- silence or a reduced soundscape at a key reveal.

Do not assign a music cue to every shot. A purposeful quiet moment can give the following sound greater impact.

## 7. Continuity checklist

Before delivery, inspect every adjacent shot pair:
- Does the geography still make sense?
- Are character positions and eyelines consistent?
- Does movement cross the cut coherently?
- Does lighting direction change accidentally?
- Are props and costume stable?
- Is the transition motivated?
- Does the new shot add information or feeling?
